# Trading town: connected development checkpoint

## Player-facing changes

Town Hall now offers a specific first decision: broker a proposed food buffer with enterprise cash and a dated delivery, or plan local food production. After delivery, the next decision compares limited export agreements. Fulfillment directs the mayor toward district commitments. These prompts link actual controls rather than granting money or food.

New towns inherit three regional approach roads as part of their founding infrastructure. They reach the original charter's northern/eastern/southern boundary, rather than stopping in interior blocks. Existing towns can review paid approaches; private titles are preserved and blocked corridors are not bulldozed. Original gateway signs identify the three connections. Outer districts and later road construction can shift where the connected network reaches the region; dedicated world-scale highway rights beyond the charter remain future work.

A shared local dispatch budget is500 units/day plus120 per attending connected warehouse worker. It competes with each outside market's finite handling capacity. A building without crews adds no capacity. This is a dispatch constraint, not a simulated individual truck queue or dedicated warehouse inventory system; loading-yard choreography and more detailed depot receipts remain further work.

Purchasers now compare local landed costs with available regional price, duties and freight. Overpriced local offers can lose business to imports; pending inventory and transit time remain real. No unlimited buyer payments are created.

Public Works includes evidence-based district prospectuses: actual woodland/water counts, river-versus-lake shipping, access burden, service-cost estimates and district-specific commitments. After buying a deed, choose:

- Productive trade park: new homes excluded, creating a commuting/infrastructure burden.
- Mixed neighbourhood: heavy industry and cargo wharves excluded; residents require reachable services.
- Working waterfront: scarce shore plots reserved for fisheries, docks and parks, with homes/civic uses inland.

Existing titles remain legal. Initial designation is free; revision requires30 days and a real$1,200 planning purchase. Qualifying connected activity must hold30 days: staffed firms, working fishery/wharf, or residents/workplaces with actual local care and schooling. Established districts unlock600-unit supplier agreements rather than200-unit starter lots. The resulting larger commitment must still be produced, shipped, financed and delivered. Completed district suppliers are prioritized when choosing eligible market offers. There is no free municipal cash or production multiplier.
