# Funded vacancy ranking under natural credit histories

Source: `dd11a82fb9f469ed58726fb7bde7a5b21d900e72` (`assets/game.js` SHA-256 `2fceb86f38436e7698be260a22baf415421c84875389c13b94f99c961976cdbc`). The reproducible browser harness is `tests/test_allocator_credit_natural_histories.py`; raw annual samples are in [`allocator-credit-natural-histories.json`](allocator-credit-natural-histories.json).

Each of seeds 42, 91 and 909 was naturally advanced for one year, then the same saved town was replayed for four years in five arms: funded-offer comparator, prior wage-only comparator, funded comparator with policy rate raised to 18% and lending rules to 90, wage-only comparator under the same tightening, and funded comparator with those policies restored after two years. Before the matched archive, the actual `bankLoan` API approved a $500, 720-day loan for a profitable firm with an open position. All arms shared the same loan and starting archive; there were no grants, forced hires, injected goods or debt write-offs.

The isolated defect has a clear natural consequence: funded-offer ranking recorded fewer unpaid worker-days in each seed. At year four, funded versus wage-only results were 7 vs 17 (seed 42), 13 vs 28 (seed 91), and 0 vs 0 (seed 909). Under credit tightening, the paired results were 7 vs 14, 7 vs 11, and 0 vs 0. This reflects better order among vacancies when available cash cannot cover all promised payroll; it does not change cash or reserve money. All 15 histories passed town validity, cash conservation (drift under $0.1), and goods conservation (error under 0.01).

The $500 firm loan was approved and repaid without a default in every arm. The selected employer subsequently maintained or filled its open roles and expanded staffing in multiple arms, so the comparator does not block legitimate financed hiring. Tightening increased that variable-rate loan from about 8.6% to about 23.2–23.6%; the normal-rate recovery arm restored policy after year two, but the matched loan was already repaid by then, so this run does not establish a rate decline on an outstanding loan. At year four, new active loans were present in baseline seed 42 and 91 histories (three and two), absent in their tight-credit arms, and present again in two recovered seed histories. These are small-sample model outcomes, not a universal access guarantee.

The towns continued growing; these interventions did not reproduce a town-wide recession or defaults. Year-four food shortfall over the previous 30 days was effectively zero in all arms. Two private providers (school and fire) remained operating and staffed in all fifteen histories; the allocator handles their eligible firm vacancies as funded jobs, while public-work assignments are managed separately. Measured public school coverage stayed near 20–23%, and measured public fire coverage was zero throughout these sampled outcomes. That service deficit is not explained or repaired by the vacancy comparator. The natural runs establish no phantom public-work priority; they do not test every private-provider entry or closure path.

Cumulative unpaid worker-days remain nonzero in some histories because employers can lose liquidity after hiring; the ranking is an offer-order rule, not a guarantee of future payroll. Realized underpayment and worker exit remain modeled outcomes. The eight workplace-isolation fixture attempts in the paired matrix could not disconnect only one of two nearby firms without also breaking the competitor's road path. Part-time jobs and commute fares are not represented in this allocator.
