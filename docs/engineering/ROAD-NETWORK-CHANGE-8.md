# Expanded-district road removal and recovery

The mayor can now retire an owned road tile through **Construction → Retire a street tile**. A tap opens a quote before commitment; the quote is revalidated on approval against the simulation day, finances, road revision and complete impact summary. Changed estimates require review again. It names affected connected homes, residents, employed household members, isolated employers and commuters, civic buildings, and households losing their current road-distance reach to a public clinic or school. The map uses a warm red highlight for a removable tile. The estimate explicitly separates street reach from funded and staffed service coverage.

The road is refused when it serves an active bus/elevated-metro route, an active shipment, a regional freight gate, or a completed/in-progress replacement-housing commitment. A permitted removal clears its road metadata and invalidates the existing road graph, traffic, distance, service and access caches. Ordinary road construction restores links at the existing $150/tile cost; removal costs $50. Cash transfers to the regional contractor account, and total money remains conserved. Residents, firms, job assignments, titles, shipment paths and public infrastructure are never silently deleted.

## Acceptance scenario

`tests/test_road_removal_network.py` uses deterministic seed 42 and real map construction controls. It buys the East Meadow parcel with the town treasury, builds two separate road connections, and establishes an outer home and productive farm with four working commuters. An ordinary $60 regional grain import is dispatched on the first trunk. A temporary replacement-housing commitment proves that the other connection is protected; its pre-commit archive is restored, leaving the test town free of that temporary project.

The live shipment keeps the first trunk protected and in use while the second road can be reviewed. The actual removal dialog reports two isolated sites, the household and two residents, four commuters to the employer, and the loss of current clinic and school road reach. The paid removal leaves the shipment's original path intact on the separate trunk. After archive save/load while disconnected and one normal simulation day, all four employees are marked commute-blocked, the home remains disconnected, the cargo shipment advances outside, and town validation passes. A normal map-built replacement road restores main-network access and the original 40-segment clinic/school distances. The archive then advances for 30 days twice with exact replay equality.

On this controlled fixture, the $50 removal is recorded as treasury `-$50`, regional contractor `+$50`, and municipal works spending `+$50`. The $150 rebuild transfers treasury `-$150` to the regional contractor. Combined cash is conserved through both actions and the 31-day disconnected/reconnected run; validation reports no goods/accounting issues. No service provider, transit vehicle, bridge, or shoreline route is created for this test. Water crossings are not part of the current road-construction mechanics, so the test stays on land. Existing transit and road-distance regression suites also pass; no transit/freight routing semantics changed.
