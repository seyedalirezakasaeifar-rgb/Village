# Public-school siting and funded access QA

The Playwright harness runs the ordinary public construction operation (`construct('school', x, y)`) against four generated cases: settlement and regional starts, seed 42, each at a nearest and far legal road-adjacent site. It does not grant cash or residents. Each build pays the ordinary $6,900 municipal cost from the generated $90,000 treasury; education is then authorized at $1,000/day for one actual service settlement. The test records legal facilities, road catchments, public staffing and paid coverage, household-level access and migration-service forecasts. It also isolates the newly placed school for a diagnostic comparison, removes its adjacent road tiles temporarily, restores them, and repeats the funded-school archive/replay for ten days. A private-school investment request is checked afterward as the market alternative.

All four towns already had one public school. This was a useful correction to the earlier zero-reachable-school assumption: zero education authorization had meant zero funded public learning, not zero school buildings or road access. After adding the second school, settlement public access and paid coverage reached 100% (280/day paid); the regional case reached 79.15% (about 861.54/day paid), bounded by accessible/staffed capacity. The balance of the $1,000 authorization was not spent. Public coverage and migration-service assessment match; the household panel and access overlay report road distance, available places, partial coverage and unserved homes.

With only the added school counted, the nearest settlement site reaches all homes; the far site reaches 65.68%. In the regional starts the added school's catchment reaches 39.57% for both selected sites because the town is more dispersed. Cutting the actual adjacent road links takes the isolated new school's access to zero in every case; restoring those same roads restores its previous isolated-school catchment. Town-wide coverage can remain supported by the pre-existing school, which is why the isolated-site comparison is reported separately. It does not claim that all-town coverage falls to zero when one school is disconnected.

The normal service-access overlay gives useful post-build feedback: green means available, gold partial, copper unserved, and it explains road distance/shared places. Household inspection shows local road segments and latest funded coverage. The construction footprint preview still marks legal placement only; it does not forecast prospective catchment before the mayor spends. That is a feedback limitation, but the runs show no accounting, forecast or migration inconsistency requiring a narrow production fix. Public education fund buys real staffing service after placement; access alone and budget authorization do not promise coverage.

Cash/goods validation passed; the funded school state survived exact ten-day save/replay in all four cases. Regional seed 42 produced a pending private-school construction offer after public authorization was removed; settlement seed 42 did not offer one. This is a real availability difference, not a guaranteed substitute. Generated starts, one seed per scenario and a ten-day replay are focused QA, not a general siting or long-term education benchmark.

Reproduce with `python tests/test_public_school_siting.py`. Full row-level outputs and the source SHA-256 are in `docs/evidence/public-school-siting8/results.json`.
