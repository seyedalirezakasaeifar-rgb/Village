## Findings requiring an owner's decision

1. **Passive prosperity remains a pacing problem.** Without a mayor purchase, the three baseline towns finish year 10 with $1.62–1.97m treasury and year 30 with $1.27–6.08m, no municipal debt, 98–100% employment and 2–6% hardship at the final observation. Intermediate hardship exists, so this is not a theorem of guaranteed prosperity. Nevertheless the observed towns need very little fiscal management to accumulate large reserves. Taxes or production should not be arbitrarily weakened to manufacture activity. The gameplay owner should use consequential district/service obligations and disclosed, recoverable supply challenges to make accumulated capacity useful.

2. **A genuine long-run monetary squeeze is hidden by ten-year tests.** Holding policy rate 18% and underwriting 90 produces year-10 employment of 92–100%, yet year-30 employment falls to 57–66%, hardship to 19–31%, and regional cash to $29.6–71.9k. The bottom household liquidity decile reaches zero in all seeds. Restoring founding credit settings after two years largely avoids that late squeeze (99.3% mean final employment). Restoring only after year 25 improves mean employment from 62.7% to 84% and hardship from 24.7% to 14%, but does not rapidly restore the regional cash pool. The high-rate and late-recovery experiments have identical measured trajectories through year 25; the validation receipt checks this explicitly. This is partial recovery, not an irreversible-death-spiral claim.

   Code inspection identifies a plausible reinforcing mechanism: `bankDaily()` pays a floating regional yield on all bank cash, while `capitalCirculationDay()` distributes surplus firm/property earnings but has no corresponding bank-equity distribution. In the seed-42 late-recovery town, banks 0/1 have zero deposit claims yet retain approximately $1.24m/$0.89m of cash. The third bank holds about $9.03m cash plus $10.19m regional credit against $19.09m deposit claims. These are conserved financial claims, not minted currency. Before changing rates or coefficients, the economics lead should test explicit regional-shareholder distributions above bank capital **and liquidity** reserves, and compare real paid credit/interest flows. That causal fix is proposed, not implemented or proven by these policy experiments. Show declining regional purchasing liquidity before shops and employers fail.

3. **Maximum wage subsidies can starve health and public teaching while headline growth looks good.** At $15 per attending firm worker/day, closing treasuries fall to $520–1,763 after the first year and public school coverage is zero. By year 10, health reaches the model floor of 25 in every seed. At year 30, mean population is 734 versus baseline 513, but mean treasury is only $2,400 versus $4.10m and hardship is 7.7% versus 3.7%. There is no detected money/loan exploit or municipal borrowing: actual transfer ordering crowds out services. A quote for the recurring subsidy bill, actual funded service shares, and a voluntary reserve/budget cap are safer first fixes than silently promising unaffordable support. Do not score population/employment alone as success.

4. **The UI prevents the requested food-contract counterfactual in an ordinary unexpanded town.** Every seed's food-only arm finds zero food offers at all 360 monthly reviews; all trajectories exactly follow baseline. `districtPreferredSupplier()` prioritizes one preferred output per market, so food producers can be absent from the three exposed offers even when they exist. This is an unavailable player choice, not evidence that exporting food cannot hurt household reserves. The logistics/UI owner should allow choosing a supported supplier/commodity, retaining normal forecasts, working stock and funding. The separate supplier-contracts arm accepts 360 normal available offers per seed and records food access, finances and delivery status; it does not fabricate food commitments. Controlled contract-reserve tests already belong to the economic lead.

5. **Normal bus investment has a staffing and operating-cost decision, not a free access bonus.** A real cross-town depot and one bus cost $16,100. All three ordinary towns buy them without a grant or forced drivers. In seed 42 the first full year records no transit operations or fares; the route eventually operates as eligible workers become available. Over 30 years its receipts are only $36.1–50.3k against $741.7–756.7k of operating expenditure. This is a low-fare public service, so cost recovery is not required; however the purchase review should disclose eligible drivers, likely opening service and recurring subsidy. `transitOperatingDay()` only recruits eligible unemployed workers and excludes people previously filling other public roles. A paid recruitment/training option can be considered by the transit owner. This experiment authors no remote homes/district: it cannot establish the benefit of reaching a new distant labour market. Existing transit-owner capacity/access fixtures remain separate evidence.

## Other comparisons and honest limits

A normally financed import bridge delivers its actual 180 food after five to six days in every seed. A normally commissioned bakery costs $3,350 municipal cash plus ordinary regional startup financing; its project meets the paid-production/sales/profit condition after 6–66 days. All six commissioned food projects complete. Their subsequent 30-year whole-town paths have different fiscal, household and population outcomes; a historical completed project does not prove that its original bakery remains open forever. Neither route consistently dominates all observed objectives. Tariff protection is tested separately from that concrete local investment.

Public emphasis raises school/fire budgets to $250 each and stops **new** private licenses. It preserves inherited providers and previously commissioned projects, so it is not a confiscation-based pure-public fixture. The private and voucher arms reduce public school/fire authorizations to zero and repeatedly invite commercially viable providers. Fees, low-wealth access, actual vouchers, provider closures, public/private training and household liquidity remain in the evidence. Vouchers are neither a guaranteed provider profit nor uniformly superior across all seeds. Public resident coverage and private learner equivalents have different denominators and must not be directly ranked as identical indices.

Education investment raises the legal daily authorization from $90 to $250, retaining normal access, staffing and learning lags. Mean adult skill at year 30 is 1.016 versus baseline 1.007. Completions also depend on courses already underway at intervention, population composition and later private entry; this aggregate is not an isolated productivity estimate. Early monthly samples are retained rather than inferring an instantaneous skill boost.

No universal dominant strategy, loan arbitrage or cash-creation exploit was established. Health, learning, distribution, employment, population and fiscal reserves give different rankings. All 1,845 validations pass; maximum absolute cash drift is $0.0002273 and maximum commodity error is 6.94e-9 units. Exact accounting does not establish fun or real-world calibration. Cash totals exclude double-counting deposit/loan claims. Regional cash changes are net external financial flows; goods accounts record cumulative import/export quantities, not gross trade-dollar turnover. Household liquidity quantiles use household-weighted per-person balances; resident-hardship-days is a proxy integrating the rounded hardship index.

Initial derived service reports clear on archive restore: use day-30 onward coverage. Births and inward migration count newly created resident identities during intervention; deaths and outward migration include the founding period and should be differenced against the initial sample. Full financial balance-sheet solvency, individual freight invoices over the entire history, human enjoyment, dense-city FPS and physical Android behavior are not certified by this laboratory. No annexations, neighbourhood relocation or mall/routing implementation was duplicated.

## Reproduction and integration

Install `requirements-test.txt` and Chromium, assemble the intended frozen integration checkpoint, then run:

```sh
python tools/balance_lab.py --years 30 --seeds 42,101,909
python tools/summarize_balance_lab.py reports/balance-lab/runs.json
```

`--modes` selects a comma-separated subset; `--out` keeps independent seed workers separate; `--resume` skips complete compatible histories while preserving interrupted attempts. Only reuse an output directory with the same warmup and production hash. New runs replace JSON atomically. The evidence was produced in independent seed/family processes to keep inference idle during simulation. Probe additions expose observations and legal interventions; they do not patch production rules. A final metadata clarification corrects the early method text's overly broad “no manual construction” phrase for the explicitly recorded paid bus/bakery purchases, retaining the original wording alongside it.

This branch is tests/tools/reports only. Cherry-pick its commits and do **not** replace the lead's newer generated bundle or run a release build on this evidence branch. Re-run selected interventions after any economic change; the retained conclusions apply to the stated frozen source. Production coefficient changes remain the economics lead's decision.
