# Food shortage and recovery check (8.0)

**Source:** `codex/8-balance-lab` at `03a5db9`; game-bundle SHA-256 `c2bf453d27bd77507f8b29c09df23187f7db216f4505e921f65cd6db9ed76447`. The deterministic browser harness and full daily observations are in [`results.json`](../evidence/food-shortage-recovery8/results.json). This is a two-seed model check, not a balance benchmark.

## Method

For each real starting town (settlement seed 42; regional seed 909), simulated 60 ordinary days and saved that checkpoint. From it, ran matched 240-day histories: (1) imports continuously enabled; (2) new import orders disabled for 120 days then enabled; and (3) imports left enabled while the three fixed external market gateway road tiles were removed from the live road graph for 120 days, then restored. The closure preserves actual graph routing and inventory; it is a deterministic test-only physical road outage, not a player UI control. Existing cargo could arrive if already beyond the affected stage, wait while routes were blocked, or enter the logistics recovery path. Both interrupted histories were replayed exactly.

The harness sampled daily inventory, empty-store days, unmet/disconnected household food demand, retailer and wholesale prices, CPI, wages, payroll, employment, retailer margins, household wallets and hardship, upstream inventory, imports/cargo, population, treasury/firms, and goods accounts. It checked connected routes before closure, no market routes during the outage, and restored routes afterward. Daily town validation and firm operating statements reconciled; total money drift per 240-day run stayed below one cent and commodity-account errors below 0.000000002 units.

## Observations

- **Settlement:** The physical closure removed all 78 reachable firm-to-market routes. For 120 days grocer stock fell 2,539 to 1,099 units; there were no unmet-food days. Restoring the 87 available routes replenished stock to 2,735, with no unmet food in that phase. The local supply chain buffered this town. The order-pause control likewise produced no unmet-food days.
- **Regional town:** The closure removed all 252 routes. Grocer stock fell 6,453 to 4,662; retailers recorded 32 empty-store-days and households had unmet food on 32 days (3,476 units). In the next 120 days, routes returned (273 initially, 285 by phase end) and grocer stock rose 5,035 to 10,160; there were no empty-store-days, but unmet demand persisted on all 120 days (14,221 units total), tapering to 17.6 units on the final day. The remaining inventories and reopened routes did not immediately clear household demand. This is a sustained recovery lag, not a fully recovered food-access outcome. The order-pause control did not produce shortages in the first 120 days; it had six unmet-food days in its following recovery phase.
- **Prices and employment did not signal scarcity reliably.** In the regional road-closure phase, retail food price fell $4.20 to $3.79, wholesale food $3.14 to $2.91, and CPI 92.3 to 90.4, despite the 32 unmet-food days. During reopened routes, they fell further (retail $3.79 to $3.61; wholesale $2.91 to $2.78; CPI 90.3 to 89.3) while unmet demand remained. Wages changed modestly ($31.63 to $31.90 during closure; $31.97 to $32.23 after reopening); employment stayed at 100%, and the measured hardship rate was zero at each phase endpoint. Treat these as observed game output, not real-world inflation claims.

## Confirmed accounting defect and fix

The road test stranded import cargo long enough to exercise the existing recovery path. If the recovered amount exceeded the cargo's book value, `pendingReturnAdjustment` was negative and the daily books subtracted it from operating expenses. Net earnings then included the recovery but the statement omitted it from income; on settlement seed 42 the reproduced statement discrepancy was $75.88. In `src/30-economy.js`, negative return adjustments are now classified as non-sales income, while positive adjustments remain expenses. Cash settlement and goods accounting are unchanged. The daily statement reconciliation now passes throughout both road histories and exact replay.

## Interpretation and limit

CPI currently reflects grocer prices (55%), rent index (25%), and a fixed remainder; it does not include food availability. A falling CPI can coexist with unmet food demand, as this regional road test demonstrates. The player should be able to see stockouts and unmet demand alongside price measures. Regional access recovered immediately at the route level, but household food demand did not fully recover during the following 120 days. Further work should explain the demand/access lag and test more town seeds before changing coefficients.

Only two seeds were run. The road cut freezes the existing external gateway locations in the harness so removing those boundary roads represents loss of external access rather than allowing the game's dynamic gateway picker to move the portal inward. It does not claim to test an in-game road-closure UI. Full daily paths, money/goods accounts, cargo backlog and replay data are retained in the evidence file.
