# Short-span road bridges

The mayor can review and build a raised public road across a straight, municipally owned span of one to five water tiles. East Meadow's enclosed lake has no one-to-three-tile crossing; its narrowest cardinal cut is five tiles (x64–68 at y15), so five is the smallest useful bounded maximum on the shipped atlas. A quote requires both banks to be owned road tiles, at least one on the town road network, an economically useful connection, and no existing building, land reservation, transit right of way, dock or active fishing/freight-water path in the span. Construction is $4,200 plus $1,850 per water tile; upkeep is $2.50 per tile per day. Unfunded upkeep degrades structural condition and closes the road graph below 25%; repairs and decommissioning are separately reviewed. Capital, upkeep and salvage transfer between existing municipal and regional accounts.

Bridge decks are stored as tile type `4` with an identity record in `G.bridges`. The water network deliberately continues to regard type `4` as water, while `roadSurface(id)` admits only structurally open bridge cells into road, traffic, transit, service and freight topology. `bridgeRenderSnapshot()` exposes `{id, axis, cells, banks, condition, closed, displayName, maintenancePerDay}` for a renderer overlay. A production deck/rail overlay must paint after water actors; the base road graph currently supplies routing but does not by itself render raised structure or clearance. No change is made to water depth or navigation ownership.

Archive validation rejects unowned banks, malformed or orphaned bridge cells, duplicate tile use, unsafe spans, bad condition, or an absent bridge record for type-4 tiles. Atlas normalization remaps both bank and span IDs. Legacy archives without bridges remain accepted. Removing either bridge bank road is refused until the bridge is retired or a replacement approach is connected; retiring a bridge also refuses active passenger or freight routes, paid shipments, and protected replacement homes. Review quotes are recomputed before charge. Closed bridges remain visible as water but do not carry traffic.

Acceptance evidence is generated by `tests/test_road_bridge.py` into `docs/previews/urban8/road-bridge.json`. The test creates the deed and road approaches with normal in-game prices, uses the construction panel and map to review/cancel/stale/re-authorize a bridge, confirms that lost title, a temporary right-of-way, active navigation, a dock footprint and insufficient capital each refuse the proposal, and verifies bridge tiles never become building plots. It confirms the water and road graphs both cross the five cells, opens an ordinary funded east-bank farm and home, sends a paid 60-unit regional cargo order across the deck, checks household health access and school-range disclosure, protects the active shipment and bank roads, validates a remapped 80-tile archive, saves and reloads the moving shipment mid-crossing, and verifies conservation and exact 30-day save replay. The same expanded town is measured before and after construction: the east-bank home begins disconnected from the clinic network and its farm has no regional quote; after the crossing, health reach is 57 steps and the paid 60-unit order has a route across all five cells. No residents, goods or money are injected; the home, farm and order use ordinary construction and market actions. A separately labelled exhausted-cash maintenance check closes the road while water stays navigable; a $6,773 funded repair restores road access with no balance drift. A paid detour case shows only a two-step saving and is declined with an explicit no-benefit reason. Repeating the original authorization cannot charge twice. `tests/test_road_removal_network.py`, `tests/test_transit_network.py`, `tests/test_regional_trade.py`, and `tests/test_logistics_recovery.py` were also rerun.
