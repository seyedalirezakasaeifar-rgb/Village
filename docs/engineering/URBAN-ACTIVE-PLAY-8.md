## Consequential findings and fixes

1. Ordinary mayor-built homes immediately create a new household. That made the instruction to provide actual vacant replacement housing unreliable as a player action. Public Works now offers **Commission replacement housing**: $20,000 buys a real 24-workday municipal home, subject to the existing crews/materials. It spawns no family, remains municipal property, and is reserved from routine voluntary mobility until used in an approved land assembly. At most eight vacant/pending replacement dwellings can be reserved at once. Occupied stock is real usable housing, not a prepaid shelter flag or an invented population.
2. A forced move could previously select the nearest private vacancy without checking future rent. Clearance now matches the least-liquid households first, prefers actual municipal replacement dwellings, and checks private rent against conservative earnings/pensions and a two-year cash buffer (including the genuine relocation allowance and owner compensation only where that household actually owns the title). If no affordable real replacement exists, clearance refuses and points to the housing commission action. Municipal replacement rent is capped at 25% of actual after-tax household income; receipts go to the treasury and $1/home/day maintenance transfers real treasury cash to suppliers. Underfunded upkeep lowers condition. The municipality does not pay itself acquisition compensation when clearing its own title.
3. Farm renewal previously replanted five fields regardless of the previous farm size. It now finds and pays to prepare enough legal replacement fields **before mutating** the farm. If the destination cannot hold the actual prior crop acreage, renewal refuses. The tested established farm keeps all 15 plots, its field multiplier, firm/building identity, stocks, workers and accounts. Crop-area preparation adds $200 per plot to the paid contract. No grain is created during relocation.
4. The voluntary mobility review advanced its 24-household window by only one household/month. The window now advances by 24, covering the population fairly instead of taking decades to reach high IDs. Reserved replacement homes cannot be taken by these ordinary moves.

## Actual-control evidence

`tests/test_urban_active_play.py` uses the mayor's replacement-housing button and authorization dialog, then actual settlement days. The commissioned dwelling finishes on day 25, stays vacant without spawning a household, and survives a save/restore. Replaying its original authorization cannot spend twice. The mayor then inspects and clears an existing home, moves the same family into the real municipal home, and builds a clinic on the former home site. A 120-day continuation preserves household identities and exact cash/goods accounting. A controlled low-income offer caps a $50 base rent to $2.50 for $10 after-tax income; zero income pays zero rent. A zero-income private market offer is refused while the real municipal offer is accepted.

Additional checks cover a protected housing reserve, malformed private ownership, public title compensation equal to zero, a 15-plot farm renewal, and a genuinely privately financed school acquisition/closure without converting it into public coverage. Provider entry waits for a real payroll day, consistent with the lead's newer economic-viability contract.

The expansion intervention buys seven sequential deeds using a clearly labeled transfer of $500,000 from the existing regional cash account, not new money. Deeds cost $250,908. Two outer road tiles and a home start on a disconnected network: utilities false, school coverage zero and no invented employment. Paying $9,150 for a legal connected corridor enables utility access while school coverage remains zero. The town then runs a further year with conserved accounts. This demonstrates the infrastructure/service opportunity cost of annexation rather than treating plot count as the gameplay result.

A 180-day busy-site audit samples 900 farm-clearance quotes: 246 correctly wait for paid cargo and 654 are available. The strict cargo guard therefore creates real timing friction without permanently locking these farms. A future disclosed wind-down control could make scheduling easier, but this follow-up does not change/duplicate logistics ownership or request a new dispatch API.
