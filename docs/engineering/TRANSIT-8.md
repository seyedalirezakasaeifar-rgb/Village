# Surface bus and elevated metro implementation contract

Public network is additive in `src/46-transit-network.js`, UI in `src/71-transit-office.js`. Existing privately funded underground remains operational and owns its own cash; new public fares transfer households → municipal treasury, with no new money account. New service replaces the old abstract commuter-budget spending when public routes exist. Explicit driver payroll uses existing unemployed residents once, before employer attendance. Fleet, route capacity, cost, road condition, headways and daily boardings determine available passenger slots. Each commuter has at most one assigned route itinerary and pays once.

Infrastructure: `transit_depot` is a municipal 2x2 footprint; `elevated_station` is a municipal 1x1 footprint with a road portal. Bus stops are owned public road cells. Both modes follow contiguous owned road rights of way; rail uses persistent elevated track above streets, not a line through properties. No private title is cleared. Initial service scope supports road corridors rather than water viaducts or tunnels.

API: `transitQuote({mode,depot,stops,level})`, `buildTransitRoute(spec,quote)`, `setTransitLevel(id,level)`, `deleteTransitRoute(id)`, `transitJourney(home,destination)`, `transitAccess(origin,destination)`, `transitRenderSnapshot()` and `transitVehiclePoses(time,zoom)`. Stops are road tile indices for buses and building IDs for elevated metro. Rail building IDs are resolved through road portals. Revision + road graph identity invalidate bounded routing/walking caches. No fixed 56/80 dimension in transit logic; cell index uses engine T.

Renderer contract: snapshot `{revision,routes:[{id,mode,color,path,stops,operating,capacity,boardings,demand,headway,reliability,status}],stations:[{id,x,y,kind}],depots:[...]}`. `path` uses engine road tile IDs; station IDs refer to buildings. Vehicle poses are visual samples only, with `{kind:'transit',mode,route,color,x,y,dx,dy,elevation,load}` in world-projected pixels. Economic capacity never depends on camera, time or visual vehicles. Far zoom returns no vehicle samples. Parent can hand this interface to art engineer.

Save validation is atomic, rejects malformed routes/IDs/duplicate assets/NaN and keeps legacy absence additive. Limited routes/stops/fleet and cached single-source road BFS avoid per-frame pathfinding. Integration hooks are small in economy, assignment, markets, save validator, map tap and construction panel/renderer. Tests and final limitations follow in the completion report.

## Completed behavior

The Public Works transit office builds municipal depots ($9,000), roadside elevated stations ($12,500), bus corridors and elevated metro corridors. Route drafts spend nothing while the mayor selects stops, depot and frequency. A current quote must be explicitly authorized; stale and repeated submissions cannot spend twice. Routes can be replanned, reduced, suspended, retired and vehicles resold. Depots have eight bays; metro sets occupy two each. An in-use station or depot cannot be scrapped. Unused facilities and guideway have an explicit salvage action; they are never silently removed.

Bus and metro vehicle capital, guideway, operating wages and upkeep are real treasury → counterparty transfers. Operating wages go to actual qualified, reachable, unemployed local residents, who are reserved from other public payroll and firm employment. There is no regional driver spawn or infinitely funded bus bonus. Bus street wear lowers reliability and increases headway. Missing cash, drivers, streets, stations or depot access reduces or suspends actual capacity. Four service levels include suspension; available vehicles are one, two or four. Existing vehicle ownership remains while service is suspended.

Each daily commuter has at most one paid public itinerary. Commuter tickets are prepaid when capacity is assigned, so the same household cash cannot fund several riders or later disappear into shopping before the fare is charged. Settlement is idempotent: the subsequent household travel loop cannot charge that ticket again. Grocery journeys consume two actual passenger slots and pay actual municipal fares. Walking uses bounded street paths, transfers are available between nearby stops, and transfers share one integrated fare. Overflow retries usable parallel routes before recording riders left behind. Passenger links do not change freight road connectivity or service-provider catchment geography.

Useful public service changes travel costs, shop choices and actual attendance on journeys beyond the walking/road commute limit. Rail does not grant free coverage to every household near one station: origin and destination need usable connected stops. Baseline towns without a public network retain their previous attendance and legacy commuter-support behavior. Public routes replace that legacy support spending while present. The budget outlook includes public route commitments and actual fare receipts; service-level controls authorize the quoted daily operations independently of the old commuter slider.

## Integration details

- `G.transit`: version 1, bounded to 48 routes × 16 stops, with vehicle ownership, guideway tile IDs, capital/operating/fare totals, current daily state and per-route capacity/denial/status/staff.
- `transitOperatingDay()` must run **after household/person daily financial fields are reset and before employer attendance/payroll**. Moving it to the end of the daily tick would pay drivers after their role had been counted elsewhere and would apply access one day late.
- `validateCivicState` must allow public role `transit`. New source types are `transit_depot` (2×2) and `elevated_station` (1×1). They are municipal property and never create an enterprise with synthetic working capital.
- `transitOperatingCommitment()` is the authorized operating ceiling. Add it to forecasts and subtract the old `transitBudget` term when public routes exist. `G.transit.fares` is cumulative municipal passenger revenue; do not add another cash account to `totalMoney()`.
- `remapTransitTiles(mapTile)` remaps **only** tracks, route path arrays and bus stop tile IDs after a region expansion. Elevated stop IDs, depot IDs, route IDs, driver IDs and stop `offsets` are institution IDs/array offsets and must remain unchanged. The caller must also invalidate the main graph after reindexing. The function clears transient topology/itinerary caches and daily commuter tickets.
- `transitInfrastructureInUse(buildingId)` protects routed depots/stations from external redevelopment. `transitRightOfWay(tileId)` protects elevated rights of way from ordinary building placement. To retire a route first use the public API, then explicitly scrap unused sites/guideway. Neither API silently clears private titles.
- Urban changes that replace a road/station portal suspend an incompatible elevated alignment until replanned; bus paths can re-route along current owned roads. Persistent unused guideway can be explicitly removed.
- UI hooks: `onTap` → `transitMapTap`, `closeBuildTool`/town reset → draft cancellation; construction panel prefix → `transitOfficePanel`. Android Back and Escape cancel map drafts. Small new source modules own almost all UI and art.
- Initial original art lives in `src/68-transit-art.js`. The graphics worker can replace `transitFacilityArt`, `drawTransitInfrastructure`, `drawTransitVehicles` while keeping poses economic-state-free. Vehicle poses stop below zoom .38. For final depth composition, move pose drawing into the scene actor pass and remove the separate `drawTransitVehicles` call; do not draw both.

Routing caches by town, topology, revision and day. Stop walks are bounded to five street tiles; transfers to three. Origin shortest-path trees are capped at 512 entries. No path search occurs in the visual pose API. Station graph nodes are bounded at 768; a binary heap handles trip routing. Paused visuals use the existing frozen frame clock. This is deterministic stylized service, not a per-passenger continuous vehicle boarding simulation.

## Verification and remaining limits

Three targeted browser suites cover genuine map clicks and quote authorization/cancel, frequency edits, stale/double submissions, depot occupancy, real fare/payroll/capital conservation, exact save continuation, malformed archives, bus/metro service, physical disruption, alternative corridors, transfers and scale. The controlled long-commute fixture has 300 actual employed residents: no operating rail permits zero to attend; one funded train permits 199 and leaves 101 behind; a second corridor carries all 300. A two-leg transfer network carries 300 riders with 600 vehicle boardings while collecting exactly $375, one integrated fare per rider.

The 48-route/768-stop/2,000-rider stress fixture pays exactly 192 unique drivers, assigns all 2,000 riders, and performs no path search during visual pose sampling. Host Chromium timings are recorded by `tests/test_transit_scale.py`; these are transit-only fixture costs, not complete-city or Android FPS measurements. Targeted captures inspect actual desktop and 390px portrait controls and original above-ground assets.

Supported scope is municipal buses and street-aligned elevated metro, with the old private underground retained. Water viaducts, tunnels, private bus franchises, schedules by hour and literal individual rider boarding are outside this implementation. School/medical catchments deliberately retain the existing separately funded road-access model: extending them through transit requires explicit paid client allocation, not a free radius bonus. The graph has no new freight links. Art is a functional original starting point for the graphics worker; final occlusion/compositing and integrated expanded-world device performance remain integration gates. No APK, signing, deployment or release changes were performed.

Final source SHA-256: `624a5cb4e7e816d5e8232b3470c65505e151330908c7de33825e4d81094b92ef`. Committed captures and result JSON are in `docs/previews/transit8/`. On that source, the 48-route fixture measured 70.7 ms median daily transit work (five samples; first build 114.7 ms) and 0.034 ms per visual-pose pair. These do not include the rest of the economy or painting. The actual source also passed core release gates (seed/render independence, exact continuation, malformed saves, paid cargo ownership, 5.6 migration, pause, traffic progress and three replicated zoning seeds), household markets, local civic services and the older-WebView API gate. The file-origin release test is restricted by this browser environment; the repository's supported loopback HTTP adapter ran it successfully. No policy or credentials were changed.

## Optimization and pure archive API follow-up

`remapTransitState(state, mapTile)` is now the pure archive API. Use **`candidate.transit = remapTransitState(candidate.transit, tileMapper)`** on the candidate town before validation/adoption. The first argument is the transit object, not the whole town. Null/undefined state remains absent. It returns a deep independent copy, transforms tracks/paths/bus-stop tile IDs only, and preserves IDs, offsets, paid fares, staff, revision and daily accounts. An invalid callback result throws without touching the input or live city. It does not consult `G`, call `transitChanged`, clear tickets, change the global stride or invalidate caches. The existing `remapTransitTiles(mapTile)` is the explicit atomic live wrapper: replace live transit only after a successful pure transform, then bump revision/invalidate live caches and tickets. Archive normalization must use the pure API, never temporarily swap `G`.

Profiling the 48-route fixture identified `transitJourney` and `transitOperatingDay` as the dominant self costs, rather than vehicle painting. The optimized implementation caches immutable origin/destination itinerary templates, indexes walk access by road cell, preserves Dijkstra trees and station graphs while actual geometry/timetable are unchanged, caches capacity masks at the one-/two-seat availability boundaries, indexes route records, and selects drivers from one ordered daily candidate pool with topology-cached depot walks. A returned ticket and its route array are always independent mutable objects. The 512 origin-tree and 4,096 itinerary bounds prevent unbounded cache growth.

Service signatures depend on actual operating routes/headways, not frame time or the calendar alone. Funding/driver changes recompute service daily and invalidate a changed timetable; road/topology and route edits replace the topology owner; capacity exhaustion/next-day reset invalidate capacity masks. Fares, wages, household cash, staff uniqueness, passenger reservation and daily fairness are unchanged. A frozen copy of the original `eb2e88c` module is the test oracle: exact comparisons cover the complete transit/accounts/ticket/staff projections at 8, 12 and 48 routes and twelve transitions (funded bus, unchanged service, exhausted/restored seats, worn pavement, no funding, street closure/reopening, route edit, reduced frequency, suspension and retirement). Tests also mutate a returned ticket to prove it cannot poison the cached template.

On matched host Chromium fixtures with 2,000 prospective riders, warm medians changed from 12.2 → 3.3 ms for eight routes, 16.5 → 3.3 ms for twelve, and 60.4 → 3.4 ms for forty-eight. First construction remains more expensive (17/21.5/58 ms respectively). These are authoritative transit calculations, not whole-game or rendering measurements. See `optimization.json`, `remap.json` and the CPU-profile reports for exact hashes/samples. Whole-game network plus the later transit art is measured separately in the next checkpoint.
