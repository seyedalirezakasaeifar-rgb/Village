# Urban growth and farm renewal journey

Historical owner narrative from the `ff9f0d0` / `fb48ad1` candidate chain. Numeric examples and the final original test list describe that source. Current independently assembled results, the added failed-day guard and complete-state replay are documented in `URBAN-GROWTH-INTEGRATION-8.md`; use its source hashes and reports for acceptance.

This is a deterministic, ordinary-resource browser journey for the urban-planning candidate. It verifies that expansion decisions have real land, road, service, title, household, and cash consequences. It does not force a market outcome or add residents, businesses, crop titles, workers, cash, or goods.

## Player-facing flow

The seed-42 settlement begins with $90,000 in municipal treasury and 169 residents. Two annexed parcels cost $30,606 and $37,368. A third deed costs $40,224 and is initially disabled because the treasury is $18,198 short. With the charter panel left open, six ordinary simulated years grow treasury to $776,301.95; the same deed button updates in place and permits the purchase without refreshing the panel. The post-purchase total city money remains within $0.00004 of its original $15,493,517.

An annexed road is not treated as automatic access. The mayor pays $2,400 for a 16-tile street corridor, $20,000 for a cottage, and $600 to register a residential block plan. Two more $20,000 cottages raise the farm block's measured residential density from 3.86 to 5.86. By day 2701, the automatic renewal rule correctly still refuses to relocate: observed housing demand is 10/100 and pressure 55/100, below the automatic 18/65 thresholds. The mayor can review a separate voluntary renewal because the block has a residential plan, density above five, modest observed demand, no active cargo or project, a complete 17-title rural replacement site, sufficient regional capital, and a safe four-unit apartment site.

The renewal review quotes $54,000 of farm-relocation construction, a $10,850 recorded-owner premium, and a $100,000 investor working reserve. It shows the source and replacement coordinates, 17 active crop titles, two workers, current inventory, and the separate $175,000 apartment project. Cancel leaves the canonical save unchanged. Approval preserves the same farm and building IDs, all 17 field titles, both workers and the quoted inventory; the old site becomes a funded 40-workday apartment project. After an ordinary year the apartment project has completed. The review makes clear that future residents and rent are not guaranteed.

Separately, an occupied home is acquired through the existing compensated assembly flow. One household of three moves to a named available replacement home, receives the quoted allowance, retains all people and two reachable worker commutes, and a clinic is built on the cleared site for $8,000. The complete city cash ledger remains conserved; the day-3061 replay checkpoint validator reports no issues and total cash drift is under $0.00005. A saved 360-day continuation replays exactly after archive load.

The same ordinary-funded journey also buys a fourth, adjoining deed at the atlas edge (`belt-102-2`, $40,224), extends a real road corridor 74 tiles to the 128 × 128 boundary ($11,100), and places a cottage at grid (125, 11). The home is 114 road steps from town hall; nearest public clinic and school are 116 and 113 road steps away, beyond the modeled 60/40-step ranges. The expansion grants no phantom civic reach: the mayor must build more local services. Before the annual continuation, the apartment project advances from 40 to 36 remaining funded workdays; save/load preserves the exact project ID and remaining work, then completes the same project during the identical 360-day replay.

## Branch and finance design notes

The current banking model has townwide accounts, deposits and loans; it does not model branch-specific staffing, customer catchments, balances, cash transfers between branches, or service capacity. A privately built branch therefore cannot yet truthfully improve local access. The bounded future design is to make a branch a service location attached to an existing bank ID, not a new balance sheet: capital and operating reserve must come from that bank's actual cash or an explicitly approved credit decision; staffed positions must hire existing reachable residents and pay wages; local access can change only when a staffed branch is open and connected; account deposits, withdrawals, interest and loans still post to the same parent institution and must never be duplicated. A future implementation would also need a closing/relocation path, branch-level archive validation, and inspection of staff, capacity, access, operating costs and the parent bank's solvency. No branch mechanics or accounting are added here.
