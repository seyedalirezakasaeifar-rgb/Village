## Architecture and delivered gameplay

`27-urban-evolution.js` owns derived spatial demand, mayoral planning, compensated clearance, household mobility, farm renewal, and retail investment. `98-urban-ui.js` wraps existing inspection and Public Works panels. Renderer descriptors and passenger integration are deliberately separate from freight/economy state.

The atlas is bounded at 128 × 128, with the five existing deeds plus sixteen additional 24–28 tile parcels: 21 successive purchases and 15,596 charterable tiles including the original town. Purchases require an adjoining deed, the exact surveyed price and actual treasury funds. They do not resize arrays or replace live property IDs. The advertised limit is explicit, rather than an infinite-world claim. Parcel prices, geography, agricultural restrictions, actual road/service access and the treasury opportunity cost remain consequential.

The first import from a 56/80 grid remaps tile-keyed data to the 128 stride. Building/person/firm/household IDs and coordinates remain unchanged. Footprints, crop titles, road wear/classes/names, private roads, project land/runways, airport runway, local freight, regional shipment/recent `path` and `activePath`, river manifests and traffic paths/gates are remapped. **`depotIndex`, depot/firm/building/contract/beneficiary IDs are not tile IDs and are never remapped.** A historical river manifest reaching its old southern edge keeps a `legacyOutlet: 56 | 80` marker: the original regional carrier's already paid itinerary remains valid. New voyages use the actual current river outlets. Restore already invalidates graph and geometry ownership through the existing hydration path.

Persistent `G.urban` version 1 contains bounded plans/history, district identities and renewal records. Schema 8 marks the atlas/urban escrow change; old supported archives remain importable. New schema-8 archives are not intended for the old application. `urbanEscrow` belongs only to a retail-centre firm project, is reserved from actual regional funds at authorization, is included in `totalMoney()`, and transfers to working capital once. It is validated separately from private-service escrow.

Planning areas are stable 8 × 8 tile blocks. Districts emerge when a block has at least three actual buildings; observed activity determines agricultural/residential/employment/market/mixed character. A changed character must persist for 90 days; emptied districts dissolve after 180. Existing property remains legal when plans change. The mayor can record market-led, residential, protected agriculture, employment or civic-reserve plans. Surveys cost $600; revisions cost $1,800 and require 90 days.

New farms and fields avoid locally urban land, following housing density, civic proximity and explicit farm-belt protection. Firm site ranking uses use-specific density, work, amenities, pollution and demand. A profitable housing-demand opportunity can fund one farm relocation at a time, after commitments settle: same enterprise, building ID, cash, employees and inventories; paid replacement/relocation works and an owner land-release premium; original four tiles become a four-unit housing construction project. A 540-day farm cooldown and project/land-use checks prevent oscillation. No new grain or household cash is created by the move. New apartment occupancy still requires the existing job/housing rules.

Observed neighbourhood income per person, rent burden and condition drive the inspector/overlay. Bands are relative to measured settled-block median income; labels never assign wages, crime or capabilities. Local demand, condition, amenities and pollution affect actual rent. At most two households per monthly review can choose better existing vacant homes after comparing affordable rent, work access and local conditions. They pay real moving costs from savings, preserve identity/ownership, and have a 180-day mobility cooldown.

Land assembly is a quoted game transaction. It pays the recorded title account, buys crop titles, funds clearance, moves tenants into real vacant connected homes, and pays twelve days' severance to displaced workers. If no real housing is available it refuses displacement. Firm liquidation uses the existing inventory-export, creditor and shareholder accounting. Firm records remain for estate/credit history; no active cargo, depot handling, crop works or trade agreements may be cleared. Stored inventory is never silently erased. The mayor then funds the replacement service separately; a civic-reserve plan prevents private infill during assembly.

Shopping centres are six-tile private `grocer` developments, with an anchor food market and loading/service court. Investors require a connected 24-road-tile catchment, finite competition, available labour, forecast operating margin and a 9% construction-plus-capital hurdle. $44,000 construction and $28,000 working capital are actually funded. Completion creates eight jobs and a regional-investor-owned firm; actual staff, bought food, household purchase/travel choices, wages/taxes and existing insolvency rules determine survival. There is no automatic customer/income grant. It is one retail enterprise, not a separately simulated lease market for every shopfront.

## Hooks and APIs for other workers

- `urbanDescriptor(x,y)` → `key, plan, density, work, pollution, amenity, landValue, agricultural, name, character, incomeBand, perPersonIncome, rentBurden, condition`. These are read-only derived descriptors; renderer actors must not mutate them. District colour is an overlay decision, not a simulation bonus.
- `building.retailCentre` and `firm.retailCentre` identify shopping centres. Footprint is 3 × 2; renderer should use this descriptor for dedicated art. `firm.retailCatchment`, `retailExpectedNet`, `jobs`, stocks, actual staff/receipts, closed status and condition describe activity. Do not render customers for an unstaffed/closed centre merely because it exists.
- `urbanPlacementReason(x,y,kind)` / existing `districtPlacementReason` enforce new-use permission. Transit adds its actual kind identifiers to the civic allow-list when integrating. Existing passenger or freight access is never inferred from a plan label.
- `urbanClearanceQuote(x,y)` → `{ok, reason}` or actual `total, compensation, demolition, relocation, severance, households, relocations, workers, fields`. `urbanClearance(x,y,quotedTotal)` rechecks the complete quote before its first mutation. Transit can inspect this result before proposing land assembly.
- `urbanRegisterPropertyGuard(building => reasonStringOrEmpty)` allows transit to protect active depots/stations/alignments. Register guards once at module load; do not modify the guard list during rendering. Existing underground stations, airport and banks/town hall are already protected. Passenger links must remain distinct from freight road connectivity.
- `urbanPlanChange(x,y,plan)`, `urbanRetailProposal(explicit)`, `urbanHousingMobility()`, `urbanFarmRenewal(demand)` are authoritative decision APIs. Only the monthly `urbanEvolutionDay()` hook runs autonomous change. No per-frame economic evolution.
- `invalidateUrban()` after external ownership/position/physical-use changes; existing building/road invalidations remain necessary. Cached spatial fields use one building/household pass and a small radius stencil per building, not full-world nearest-building scans per actor. One ownership mask costs 16 KiB, four spatial fields cost 256 KiB, and the existing route-tree cap costs at most 6 MiB at this grid size.

Shared source hooks: `00-foundation` T; `05-systems` schema; `18-region-charter` bounded atlas/migration/ownership mask; `21-waterfront` historical outlet validation; `24-district-programmes` count bound; `20-world` passenger right-of-way protection; `30-economy` rent multiplier; `40-development` generic business probes, site ranking, crop siting, retail escrow/commissioning; `50-clock` monthly urban review and **RNG snapshot after investment decisions**; `65-landscape` one overlay call plus outer-terrain retention; `70-interaction` corrected placement-message precedence; `80-civic` retail escrow in cash totals; `90-archive-panels` validation; `98-charter-ui` atlas sizing and label culling. Preserve newer main/graphics/transit changes while applying these hooks.

Graphics integration: retain `drawUrbanOverlay(ctx, visible)` after charter boundaries. The expanded atlas initially caused a dense-world ground redraw regression; the branch caches the unchanged unowned outer ground in the existing bitmap (no second full-world bitmap), repaints chartered ground on change, and invalidates that retained base for town/season/quality/scale changes. Cold terrain and purchased lots still draw normally. Ownership lookup uses a revision-keyed 16 KiB mask. Keep this optimisation or an equivalent chunk/incremental strategy when merging the newer graphics work. No economic population is culled with visual LOD.

## Evidence and limits

`tests/test_urban_evolution.py` runs the assembled browser game and actual controls. It covers all 21 deeds, detached/stale/unfunded/duplicate purchases, full-atlas restore, live 80-grid cargo/active-path/crop migration, displacement refusal and real rehousing, farm closure/severance/stock accounting, intact farm relocation with four replacement dwellings, paid centre escrow/commissioning, plan and district lifecycle cooldowns, actual planning/annexation/clearance-tool controls, screenshots, and two 1,080-day replays with conservation checks. The retail and redevelopment fixtures are controlled opportunities, not claims that every passive town reaches those thresholds.

Core release gates cover render-independent replay/continuation, atomic schema rejection, zoning sensitivity, retained blocked cargo, 56-grid migration, paused frame stability and traffic progress. The 2,000-resident / 713-building host gate checks real RAF tail, simulation speed, paint and UI response. Final reports carry the assembled source SHA. Earlier renderer regressions are documented separately, not hidden by loosening acceptance thresholds. Full integrated/native acceptance and a multi-hour human playthrough remain the integration lead's final gate.

Limits: bounded 128 atlas, not unbounded geography; block-based planning, not arbitrary drawn polygons; compensated clearance requires existing vacant housing, with no invented shelter population; no multi-owner parcel purchase auction or individual shop lease economy. Retail forecast coefficients are gameplay rules and can be wrong, so funded centres can fail. Road access remains authoritative until the transit worker explicitly integrates passenger fares/access. APK signing, deployment and main-branch merges are not part of this branch.
