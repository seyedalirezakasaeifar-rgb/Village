# Three mayor strategies: three simulated years each

These browser-control traces used seed 42, the ordinary founding treasury, actual policy/build/trade controls, and real save/load. No cash, people, land or inventory was injected. They cover simulated days 1–1081, not hours of human play. Source `assets/game.js` SHA-256 at trace time: `8872d19163eb8a76c1e4cb529092febe16bfd8a5ad63b6028df35bc28a4c57bb` (before the subsequent committed-day save fix).

| Strategy | Day 1081 | Decisions and observed tradeoffs |
|---|---|---|
| Service first | 237 residents, 100% employment, 0% hardship, $156,190 treasury; measured health coverage 97.5%, school funding/access 73.9%. | On day 1, built a clinic ($8,000) and school ($6,900), then raised daily health and education allocations from $70/$90 to $170/$190. Revisited funding twice. Coverage started around 65%/31% and rose to 97.9%/61.0% by day 361 and 98.2%/66.4% by day 721. Expansion remained constrained by staffing/catchment: more cash alone did not instantly provide full school access. |
| Export and industry first | 233 residents, 99% employment, 0% hardship, $370,622 treasury; health coverage 48.8%, school 23.3%; three export agreements fulfilled. | Reviewed a paid 60-unit food import (enterprise paid about $203, nine simulated days in transit), accepted an export agreement, and built a sawmill for $4,100. The first offer showed no current surplus (zero estimated deliverable), so the new mill became a real commitment to future production. Later reviews showed 131–143 units/day output against a 200-unit agreement and estimated inventory margins around $500 before payroll/tax. Contracts fulfilled; public-service coverage fell as population grew without matching service investment. |
| Housing and transit first | 219 residents, 99% employment, 0% hardship, $249,922 treasury; health coverage 53.2%, school 41.4%. | Built a home ($20,000) and transit depot ($9,000). Reviewed a real bus proposal: 13 households/33 residents and 57 filled jobs in the walk catchment, but zero cash-affordable work trips, zero expected fares, and $69.10/day public funding. Deferred the route before paying its $7,100 capital cost. At day 361, bought East Meadow for $30,606, chartered it for neighbourhood use and commissioned one $150 road tile toward a 17-tile surveyed connection; remaining streets and new services remained separate work. Built two further homes for $20,000 each. Population grew, but service coverage declined without funded service expansion. |

All three runs preserved the cash ledger within $0.00003 and passed town validation. The journeys produced distinct strategic outcomes and meaningful costs, while also showing that transport forecasts can recommend deferral and expansion does not automatically bring residents or services. These traces document observable pacing and choices; they do not establish subjective multi-hour enjoyment.
